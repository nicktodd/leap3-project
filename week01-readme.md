# Week 1 - Kickoff Day Guidance

## What each track has been learning

**Software Engineering track:** Foundations: Agile and Scrum fundamentals and ceremonies, Git
fundamentals and branching strategies, GitHub, pull requests and repository hygiene, peer and code
review, GitHub Copilot as a learning aid, and setting up the team repository and branching
strategy.

**Systems Analyst track:** Foundations and analysis essentials: the systems analyst's role across
the SDLC, the trading platform scenario (the same business requirements spec the project uses),
ways-of-working fundamentals and drafting a team agreement, JIRA and Confluence for analysts, and
Git and repository literacy, ending with a scenario briefing pack.

> **The deliverables are fixed; how you get there isn't.** The kickoff panel expects the three
> deliverables below, but how your team organises the day, and who does what, is up to you.
> From week 2 onwards, each week's guide is a set of suggestions matched to what's just been
> taught, not a plan: your team manages its own project and the flow of its work.

## Context

Week 1's Friday is the kickoff day. The business requirements specification is issued, and each
team reads it, produces a candidate architecture, an initial project plan and a risk list, and
presents them to a panel playing the programme's stakeholders that afternoon. The slides, agenda
and the spec itself are in [`kickoff/`](kickoff).

This is the first time the engineers and analysts work together as one team, so it's also the
day the team starts working out how it'll run.

## Analyst focus

Your analysts arrive with a head start: their track has already introduced the trading platform
scenario, and they've produced a briefing pack on it. That's worth using in the first hour, while
everyone else is reading the spec for the first time.

The kickoff slides ask for two things analysts are well placed to lead. One is showing how the
design meets the trust, audit and reporting expectations in the spec, not only the visible
features. The other is flagging anything in the spec the team is still unsure how to satisfy,
alongside the questions a real programme sponsor would expect to be asked.

## Handoffs

- **Analysts bring:** the scenario briefing pack, and a first read of who the stakeholders are
  and what each one needs.
- **Engineers lead, analysts challenge:** the candidate architecture and the technology choices,
  checked against sections 8 and 9 of the spec.
- **Joint:** the initial project plan and the risk list.
- **Analysts lead:** the open questions and unclear points in the spec, captured in the risk list
  or put to your instructor during the day.

## The trap to avoid

- An architecture-only kickoff. Engineers will naturally spend the morning drawing boxes; the
  panel is playing business stakeholders and will want to hear the business outcome first.
- Two ways-of-working agreements. Both tracks drafted one this week. Merge them into a single team
  agreement early in week 2 rather than running on whichever one someone remembers. The analyst
  track reviews it again in week 10.

## What to present

The three deliverables from the kickoff slides, presented to the stakeholder panel in up to 20
minutes including questions:

- A candidate architecture, the technologies you'd use, and why.
- An initial project plan: what has to come first, what could run in parallel, and what you don't
  yet know enough to plan.
- A risk list, including anything in the spec you're still unsure how to satisfy.

Lead with the business outcome, and make sure both engineers and analysts present a real part of
it.
